# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Delegated: a dependency-light static HTML, CSS, and JavaScript build was selected so the experience can deploy directly to GitHub Pages while supporting custom motion, Canvas effects, and progressive enhancement.

## Users

The primary audience is people who remember 1990s console culture, game magazines, school binders, video rentals, dial-up internet, and early fan sites. They should be able to follow a fictional lost-media discovery without needing prior context.

## Product Purpose

Present the supplied images and video as a guided narrative about a supposedly lost Sega 32X cybersecurity game discovered in an abandoned computer store, then powered on and played. Success means the visitor understands the discovery-to-gameplay story, wants to continue through every chapter, and remembers the tactile 1990s experience.

## Positioning

The artifact behaves like an obsessively customized 1990s teenager's gaming binder and impossible future-built promotional demo, using controller navigation and changing physical materials to turn a sequential image collection into a playable story.

## Operating Context

The experience is a single-page, browser-based journey. Visitors may scroll normally or use Genesis-style A, B, C, and Start controls to inspect, return, advance, and select chapters. It must work on desktop and mobile and remain usable with keyboard navigation and reduced motion.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Static deployment compatible with GitHub Pages.
- Preserve the supplied story order and all supplied images and video.
- Alternate image/text alignment by chapter; on small screens stack image, copy, and controls.
- Never place interface controls, narrative copy, or decorative stickers over the supplied images.
- No carousel.
- Discovery chapters use dusty, distressed, physical materials.
- Gameplay chapters each receive two different 1990s youth-culture stickers, their own palette, and their own texture while remaining part of one Genesis-grid system.
- Provide controller, keyboard, pointer, and native-scroll navigation.
- Optional audio must begin muted.

## Brand Commitments

The work is an unofficial fictional fan project titled **CYBERSECURITY: Enter the Net. Defend the Future.** Its identity is tied to Sega Genesis/32X hardware, packaging geometry, a large glowing console power light, black retail grids, yellow 32X spines, chrome marks, worn game magazines, and exuberant 1990s binder culture. The intended personality is sincere, maximal, excited, tactile, and slightly haunted—not ironic minimalism.

## Evidence on Hand

The user supplied eighteen sequential PNG story assets, one enemy sprite sheet, one short MP4 gameplay clip, and four visual references showing Sega Genesis/32X packaging, controller hardware, retail grids, typography, and console power-light details. No commercial performance claims, review quotations, or verified publication history may be invented.

## Product Principles

1. The supplied artwork leads; interface and decoration occupy adjacent physical space.
2. Interaction should feel like handling hardware, paper, plastic, stickers, and a treasured binder.
3. Complexity escalates with the story: physical discovery becomes digital corruption, then resolves.
4. Exuberance is intentional, but navigation and reading remain immediately understandable.
5. Every effect must degrade gracefully for mobile, reduced motion, and lower-powered devices.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use semantic controls, visible focus states, descriptive alt text, sufficient text contrast, keyboard equivalents, reduced-motion behavior, and no sound-dependent information. Decorative cultural marks receive empty alternative text; narrative images receive meaningful descriptions.
